3 THINGS YOU NEED TO KNOW BEFORE
STARTING POWERLIFTING

L
J
Liv Dake & Jacob Reed
July 2026 5 min read

Powerlifting is one of the most rewarding sports you can get into — but there are a few things we wish every new lifter knew before they started.

You don't need to be strong. You don't need to know how to squat, bench, or deadlift perfectly. And you definitely don't need to step onto a platform anytime soon.

You just need to be willing to learn, be consistent, and enjoy the process.

Here are three things we'd want every beginner to know before starting powerlifting.

1. You Don't Need to Be Strong to Start

This might sound obvious, but it's one of the biggest barriers we see for new lifters.

Powerlifting is not just for people who are already strong.

Everyone starts somewhere.

Your first squat might feel awkward. Your bench might be inconsistent. You might have no idea how to set up for a deadlift. That's completely normal.

Strength is something you develop through training — not something you need to have before you begin.

One of the best things about powerlifting is that your progress is measurable. You can start with an empty bar or whatever weight you can comfortably manage, and gradually build from there. Your starting numbers don't determine your potential.

What matters much more is whether you're willing to show up consistently and improve over time.

2. Technique Doesn't Need to Be Perfect

If you've spent any time online looking at powerlifting content, you've probably seen a lot of conflicting advice.

"Your elbows are too flared."   "You need to prehinge your squat."   "Your feet need to be exactly here."   "Your grip has to be this wide."

It can make starting powerlifting feel much more complicated than it needs to be.

The reality is that there isn't one perfect technique that works for everyone. Your anatomy, mobility, proportions, experience, and individual goals will all influence how you perform the squat, bench press, and deadlift.

That doesn't mean technique doesn't matter — it absolutely does. But when you're starting out, the goal isn't to find a theoretically perfect position and never change it.

The goal is to develop a safe, repeatable, and effective technique that you can progressively load over time.

Your technique will also change as you become stronger and more experienced. That's a good thing. You should expect to experiment, learn, make adjustments, and refine your technique as you train.

Don't let the pursuit of perfect technique stop you from actually training.

3. Progress Isn't Linear

This is probably the most important thing to understand before starting powerlifting.

At first, progress can feel almost effortless. You might add weight to the bar every week. Your technique improves quickly. Exercises that once felt difficult suddenly become easy.

Eventually, that slows down.

You'll have workouts where the weight feels heavier than it should. You'll miss a lift. You'll have a bad day. You might even go through weeks where your numbers don't move at all.

That's normal.

Strength isn't a straight line upward. Sleep, stress, nutrition, fatigue, training experience, and countless other factors can affect how you perform on any given day.

  • A bad session doesn't mean your program isn't working
  • A missed lift doesn't mean you've gotten weaker
  • A week without a PR doesn't mean you're failing

The goal isn't to PR every workout. The goal is to continue building momentum to push your strength forward over months and years.

If you can learn to focus on the long term instead of chasing constant PRs, you'll be much better equipped to enjoy powerlifting.

So, Should You Start Powerlifting?

If you've been thinking about trying powerlifting, you don't need to wait until you're stronger, more experienced, or more confident.

Start where you are. Learn the lifts. Train consistently. Ask questions. Make mistakes. Get stronger. And most importantly — enjoy the process.

Powerlifting is a long-term sport. The people who get the most out of it aren't necessarily the people who start the strongest. They're the people who stick around long enough to see what they're capable of.

Your first goal doesn't need to be lifting a huge amount of weight. It can simply be becoming a better lifter than you were when you started.
